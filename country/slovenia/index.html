How Slovenia makes its electricity

Slovenia generated about 15 TWh of electricity in 2025. About 40% came from nuclear, and about a quarter from hydro. Low-carbon sources (nuclear and renewables) supplied about 80% of the total.

The mix in 2025

  • Nuclear
  • Hydro
  • Coal
  • Solar
  • Gas
  • Bioenergy

Each person uses about twice the world average amount of electricity. Each kilowatt-hour emits about 180 grams of CO₂, about a third of the world average.

Since 2000

Since 2000, coal fell from about a third to about 15% and solar rose from almost nothing to about 10% of the mix.

Trade and resources

Slovenia is a net exporter: it sells about 3% of what it generates to its neighbours.

  • Sunshine: about three-quarters of the world average.
  • Rainfall: close to the world average.
  • Biggest electricity trade partners: Austria, Italy, Croatia.
  • Main fuel supplier: Italy.
