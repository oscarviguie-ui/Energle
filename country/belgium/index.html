How Belgium makes its electricity

Belgium generated about 67 TWh of electricity in 2025. About 35% came from nuclear, and about 20% from wind. Low-carbon sources (nuclear and renewables) supplied about 80% of the total.

The mix in 2025

  • Nuclear
  • Wind
  • Gas
  • Solar
  • Bioenergy
  • Hydro
  • Other Fossil

Each person uses about twice the world average amount of electricity. Each kilowatt-hour emits about 110 grams of CO₂, about a quarter of the world average.

Since 2000

Since 2000, nuclear fell from about 60% to about 35%, wind rose from almost nothing to about 20% and solar rose from almost nothing to about 15% of the mix.

Trade and resources

Belgium is a net importer: about 15% of the electricity it uses comes from abroad.

  • Sunshine: about two-thirds of the world average.
  • Rainfall: about three-quarters of the world average.
  • Biggest electricity trade partners: France, the Netherlands, Germany.
  • Main fuel supplier: the Netherlands.
