How North Macedonia makes its electricity

North Macedonia generated about 7.1 TWh of electricity in 2025. About a third came from solar, and about 30% from coal. Low-carbon sources (nuclear and renewables) supplied about 55% of the total.

The mix in 2025

  • Solar
  • Coal
  • Hydro
  • Gas
  • Wind
  • Other Fossil

Each person uses a little more than the world average amount of electricity. Each kilowatt-hour emits about 410 grams of CO₂, close to the world average.

Since 2000

Since 2000, coal fell from about three-quarters to about 30%, solar rose from almost nothing to about a third and gas rose from almost nothing to about 15% of the mix.

Trade and resources

North Macedonia is a net importer: about 15% of the electricity it uses comes from abroad.

  • Sunshine: about three-quarters of the world average.
  • Rainfall: about half the world average.
  • Biggest electricity trade partners: Greece, Bulgaria, Serbia.
  • Main fuel supplier: Greece.
