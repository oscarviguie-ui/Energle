How Jordan makes its electricity

Jordan generated about 24 TWh of electricity in 2024. About 60% came from gas, and about 15% from solar. Low-carbon sources (nuclear and renewables) supplied about a quarter of the total.

The mix in 2024

  • Gas
  • Solar
  • Other Fossil
  • Wind

Each person uses about half the world average amount of electricity. Each kilowatt-hour emits about 530 grams of CO₂, a little more than the world average.

Since 2000

Since 2000, other fossil fuels fell from about 90% to about 15%, gas rose from about 10% to about 60% and solar rose from almost nothing to about 15% of the mix.

Trade and resources

Jordan makes roughly as much electricity as it uses, with little net trade.

  • Sunshine: a little more than the world average.
  • Rainfall: about a tenth of the world average.
  • Biggest electricity trade partners: Egypt.
  • Main fuel supplier: the United States.
