How the Dominican Republic makes its electricity

The Dominican Republic generated about 23 TWh of electricity in 2025. About 30% came from coal, and about a quarter from gas. Low-carbon sources (nuclear and renewables) supplied about a quarter of the total.

The mix in 2025

  • Coal
  • Gas
  • Other Fossil
  • Solar
  • Hydro
  • Wind
  • Bioenergy

Each person uses about half the world average amount of electricity. Each kilowatt-hour emits about 540 grams of CO₂, a little more than the world average.

Since 2000

Since 2000, other fossil fuels fell from about 95% to about 20%, coal rose from almost nothing to about 30% and gas rose from almost nothing to about a quarter of the mix.

Trade and resources

The Dominican Republic makes roughly as much electricity as it uses, with little net trade.

  • Sunshine: close to the world average.
  • Rainfall: about 1.5 times the world average.
  • Main fuel supplier: the United States.
