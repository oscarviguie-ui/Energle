How Japan makes its electricity

Japan generated about 1,000 TWh of electricity in 2025. About a third came from gas, and about a third from coal. Low-carbon sources (nuclear and renewables) supplied about a third of the total.

The mix in 2025

  • Gas
  • Coal
  • Solar
  • Nuclear
  • Hydro
  • Bioenergy
  • Other Fossil
  • Wind

Each person uses about twice the world average amount of electricity. Each kilowatt-hour emits about 480 grams of CO₂, close to the world average.

Since 2000

Since 2000, nuclear fell from about 30% to about 9%, other fossil fuels fell from about 15% to about 2% and coal rose from about 20% to about a third of the mix.

Trade and resources

Japan makes roughly as much electricity as it uses, with little net trade.

  • Sunshine: about three-quarters of the world average.
  • Rainfall: about 1.5 times the world average.
  • Main fuel supplier: the United Arab Emirates.
