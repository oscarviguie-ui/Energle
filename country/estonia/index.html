How Estonia makes its electricity

Estonia generated about 6.2 TWh of electricity in 2025. About 40% came from other fossil fuels, and about 20% from bioenergy. Low-carbon sources (nuclear and renewables) supplied about 60% of the total.

The mix in 2025

  • Other Fossil
  • Bioenergy
  • Solar
  • Wind
  • Gas

Each person uses about 1.5 times the world average amount of electricity. Each kilowatt-hour emits about 320 grams of CO₂, about three-quarters of the world average.

Since 2000

Since 2000, other fossil fuels fell from about 95% to about 40%, bioenergy rose from almost nothing to about 20% and solar rose from almost nothing to about 20% of the mix.

Trade and resources

Estonia is a net importer: about 30% of the electricity it uses comes from abroad.

  • Sunshine: about half the world average.
  • Rainfall: about half the world average.
  • Biggest electricity trade partners: Finland, Latvia, Russia.
  • Main fuel supplier: Lithuania.
