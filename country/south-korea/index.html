How South Korea makes its electricity

South Korea generated about 620 TWh of electricity in 2025. About a third came from coal, and about 30% from nuclear. Low-carbon sources (nuclear and renewables) supplied about 40% of the total.

The mix in 2025

  • Coal
  • Nuclear
  • Gas
  • Solar
  • Bioenergy
  • Other Fossil
  • Hydro
  • Wind

Each person uses about 3 times the world average amount of electricity. Each kilowatt-hour emits about 420 grams of CO₂, close to the world average.

Since 2000

Since 2000, gas rose from about 10% to about 30%, other fossil fuels fell from about 10% to almost nothing and coal fell from about 40% to about a third of the mix.

Trade and resources

South Korea makes roughly as much electricity as it uses, with little net trade.

  • Sunshine: about three-quarters of the world average.
  • Rainfall: a little more than the world average.
  • Main fuel supplier: Saudi Arabia.
