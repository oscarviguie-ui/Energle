How Mexico makes its electricity

Mexico generated about 360 TWh of electricity in 2025. About 60% came from gas, and about 9% from other fossil fuels. Low-carbon sources (nuclear and renewables) supplied about a quarter of the total.

The mix in 2025

  • Gas
  • Other Fossil
  • Hydro
  • Solar
  • Wind
  • Coal
  • Nuclear
  • Bioenergy

Each person uses about three-quarters of the world average amount of electricity. Each kilowatt-hour emits about 470 grams of CO₂, close to the world average.

Since 2000

Since 2000, gas rose from about 20% to about 60%, other fossil fuels fell from about 45% to about 9% and hydro fell from about 15% to about 8% of the mix.

Trade and resources

Mexico makes roughly as much electricity as it uses, with little net trade.

  • Sunshine: a little more than the world average.
  • Rainfall: about three-quarters of the world average.
  • Biggest electricity trade partners: the United States, Guatemala.
  • Main fuel supplier: the United States.
