How Canada makes its electricity

Canada generated about 650 TWh of electricity in 2025. About 55% came from hydro, and about 20% from gas. Low-carbon sources (nuclear and renewables) supplied about three-quarters of the total.

The mix in 2025

  • Coal
  • Bioenergy
  • Solar
  • Other Fossil

Each person uses about 4 times the world average amount of electricity. Each kilowatt-hour emits about 190 grams of CO₂, about a third of the world average.

Since 2000

Since 2000, coal fell from about 20% to about 4%, gas rose from about 6% to about 20% and wind rose from almost nothing to about 8% of the mix.

Trade and resources

Canada is a net exporter: it sells about 1% of what it generates to its neighbours.

  • Sunshine: about two-thirds of the world average.
  • Rainfall: about half the world average.
  • Biggest electricity trade partners: the United States.
  • Main fuel supplier: the United States.
