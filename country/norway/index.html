How Norway makes its electricity

Norway generated about 160 TWh of electricity in 2025. About 90% came from hydro, and about 8% from wind. Low-carbon sources (nuclear and renewables) supplied almost all of the total.

The mix in 2025

  • Hydro
  • Wind
  • Gas

Each person uses about 6 times the world average amount of electricity. Each kilowatt-hour emits about 30 grams of CO₂, less than a tenth of the world average.

Since 2000

Since 2000, hydro fell from almost all to about 90% and wind rose from almost nothing to about 8% of the mix.

Trade and resources

Norway is a net exporter: it sells about 15% of what it generates to its neighbours.

  • Sunshine: about half the world average.
  • Rainfall: about 1.5 times the world average.
  • Biggest electricity trade partners: Sweden, the United Kingdom, Denmark.
  • Main fuel supplier: Sweden.
