How Kenya makes its electricity

Kenya generated about 14 TWh of electricity in 2025. About 45% came from other renewables, and about 20% from hydro. Low-carbon sources (nuclear and renewables) supplied about 90% of the total.

The mix in 2025

  • Other Renewables
  • Hydro
  • Wind
  • Other Fossil
  • Solar
  • Bioenergy

Each person uses less than a tenth of the world average amount of electricity. Each kilowatt-hour emits about 100 grams of CO₂, about a fifth of the world average.

Since 2000

Since 2000, other fossil fuels fell from about 60% to about 10%, other renewables rose from about 10% to about 45% and wind rose from almost nothing to about 15% of the mix.

Trade and resources

Kenya is a net importer: about 10% of the electricity it uses comes from abroad.

  • About 100% of people have electricity at home.
  • Sunshine: a little more than the world average.
  • Rainfall: about half the world average.
  • Biggest electricity trade partners: Ethiopia, Uganda.
  • Main fuel supplier: the United Arab Emirates.
