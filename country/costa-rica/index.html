How Costa Rica makes its electricity

Costa Rica generated about 13 TWh of electricity in 2025. About three-quarters came from hydro, and about 10% from wind. Low-carbon sources (nuclear and renewables) supplied almost all of the total.

The mix in 2025

  • Hydro
  • Wind
  • Other Renewables
  • Solar

Each person uses about two-thirds of the world average amount of electricity. Each kilowatt-hour emits about 20 grams of CO₂, less than a tenth of the world average.

Since 2000

Since 2000, wind rose from about 3% to about 10% and hydro fell from about 80% to about three-quarters of the mix.

Trade and resources

Costa Rica is a net exporter: it sells about 3% of what it generates to its neighbours.

  • Sunshine: close to the world average.
  • Rainfall: about 2.5 times the world average.
  • Biggest electricity trade partners: Guatemala, El Salvador.
  • Main fuel supplier: the United States.
