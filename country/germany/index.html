How Germany makes its electricity

Germany generated about 500 TWh of electricity in 2025. About a quarter came from wind, and about 20% from coal. Low-carbon sources (nuclear and renewables) supplied about 60% of the total.

The mix in 2025

  • Wind
  • Coal
  • Solar
  • Gas
  • Bioenergy
  • Hydro
  • Other Fossil

Each person uses about 1.5 times the world average amount of electricity. Each kilowatt-hour emits about 330 grams of CO₂, about three-quarters of the world average.

Since 2000

Since 2000, coal fell from about half to about 20%, nuclear fell from about 30% to almost nothing and wind rose from about 2% to about a quarter of the mix.

Trade and resources

Germany is a net importer: about 4% of the electricity it uses comes from abroad.

  • Sunshine: about two-thirds of the world average.
  • Rainfall: about two-thirds of the world average.
  • Biggest electricity trade partners: France, Austria, the Netherlands.
  • Main fuel supplier: Norway.
