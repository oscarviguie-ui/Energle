How the United Kingdom makes its electricity

The United Kingdom generated about 290 TWh of electricity in 2025. About a third came from gas, and about 30% from wind. Low-carbon sources (nuclear and renewables) supplied about two-thirds of the total.

The mix in 2025

  • Gas
  • Wind
  • Bioenergy
  • Nuclear
  • Solar
  • Other Fossil
  • Hydro

Each person uses a little more than the world average amount of electricity. Each kilowatt-hour emits about 220 grams of CO₂, about half the world average.

Since 2000

Since 2000, coal fell from about a third to almost nothing, wind rose from almost nothing to about 30% and bioenergy rose from about 1% to about 15% of the mix.

Trade and resources

The United Kingdom is a net importer: about 9% of the electricity it uses comes from abroad.

  • Sunshine: about half the world average.
  • Rainfall: close to the world average.
  • Biggest electricity trade partners: France, Norway, Belgium.
  • Main fuel supplier: Norway.
