How the United States makes its electricity

The United States generated about 4,500 TWh of electricity in 2025. About 40% came from gas, and about 15% from nuclear. Low-carbon sources (nuclear and renewables) supplied about 45% of the total.

The mix in 2025

  • Gas
  • Nuclear
  • Coal
  • Wind
  • Solar
  • Hydro
  • Bioenergy
  • Other Fossil

Each person uses about 3.5 times the world average amount of electricity. Each kilowatt-hour emits about 380 grams of CO₂, about three-quarters of the world average.

Since 2000

Since 2000, coal fell from about half to about 15%, gas rose from about 15% to about 40% and wind rose from almost nothing to about 10% of the mix.

Trade and resources

The United States makes roughly as much electricity as it uses, with little net trade.

  • Sunshine: close to the world average.
  • Rainfall: about two-thirds of the world average.
  • Biggest electricity trade partners: Canada, Mexico.
  • Main fuel supplier: Canada.
