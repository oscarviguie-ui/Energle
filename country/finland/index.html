How Finland makes its electricity

Finland generated about 82 TWh of electricity in 2025. About 40% came from nuclear, and about a quarter from wind. Low-carbon sources (nuclear and renewables) supplied about 95% of the total.

The mix in 2025

  • Nuclear
  • Wind
  • Hydro
  • Bioenergy
  • Other Fossil
  • Solar
  • Gas

Each person uses about 4 times the world average amount of electricity. Each kilowatt-hour emits about 60 grams of CO₂, about a tenth of the world average.

Since 2000

Since 2000, wind rose from almost nothing to about a quarter, gas fell from about 15% to about 1% and coal fell from about 10% to almost nothing of the mix.

Trade and resources

Finland is a net importer: about 6% of the electricity it uses comes from abroad.

  • Sunshine: about half the world average.
  • Rainfall: about half the world average.
  • Biggest electricity trade partners: Sweden, Estonia, Norway.
  • Main fuel supplier: Norway.
