How Greece makes its electricity

Greece generated about 58 TWh of electricity in 2025. About 40% came from gas, and about 20% from wind. Low-carbon sources (nuclear and renewables) supplied about half of the total.

Each person uses about 1.5 times the world average amount of electricity. Each kilowatt-hour emits about 320 grams of CO₂, about three-quarters of the world average.

Since 2000

Since 2000, coal fell from about two-thirds to about 5%, gas rose from about 10% to about 40% and wind rose from almost nothing to about 20% of the mix.

Trade and resources

Greece is a net exporter: it sells about 4% of what it generates to its neighbours.

  • Sunshine: close to the world average.
  • Rainfall: about half the world average.
  • Biggest electricity trade partners: North Macedonia, Bulgaria, Italy.
  • Main fuel supplier: Iraq.
