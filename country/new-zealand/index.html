How New Zealand makes its electricity

New Zealand generated about 43 TWh of electricity in 2025. About 55% came from hydro, and about 20% from other renewables. Low-carbon sources (nuclear and renewables) supplied about 90% of the total.

The mix in 2025

  • Hydro
  • Other Renewables
  • Wind
  • Gas
  • Other Fossil
  • Bioenergy
  • Coal
  • Solar

Each person uses about twice the world average amount of electricity. Each kilowatt-hour emits about 90 grams of CO₂, about a fifth of the world average.

Since 2000

Since 2000, gas fell from about a quarter to about 9%, other renewables rose from about 7% to about 20% and wind rose from almost nothing to about 10% of the mix.

Trade and resources

New Zealand makes roughly as much electricity as it uses, with little net trade.

  • Sunshine: about three-quarters of the world average.
  • Rainfall: about 1.5 times the world average.
  • Main fuel supplier: South Korea.
