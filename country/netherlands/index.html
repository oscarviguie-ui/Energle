How the Netherlands makes its electricity

The Netherlands generated about 130 TWh of electricity in 2025. About a third came from gas, and about a quarter from wind. Low-carbon sources (nuclear and renewables) supplied about 55% of the total.

The mix in 2025

  • Gas
  • Wind
  • Solar
  • Coal
  • Bioenergy
  • Other Fossil
  • Nuclear

Each person uses about 1.5 times the world average amount of electricity. Each kilowatt-hour emits about 250 grams of CO₂, about half the world average.

Since 2000

Since 2000, wind rose from almost nothing to about a quarter, gas fell from about 60% to about a third and solar rose from almost nothing to about 20% of the mix.

Trade and resources

The Netherlands is a net exporter: it sells about 10% of what it generates to its neighbours.

  • Sunshine: about two-thirds of the world average.
  • Rainfall: about three-quarters of the world average.
  • Biggest electricity trade partners: Germany, Belgium, the United Kingdom.
  • Main fuel supplier: the United States.
