How Denmark makes its electricity

Denmark generated about 35 TWh of electricity in 2025. About 60% came from wind, and about 20% from bioenergy. Low-carbon sources (nuclear and renewables) supplied about 95% of the total.

The mix in 2025

  • Wind
  • Bioenergy
  • Solar
  • Other Fossil

Each person uses about twice the world average amount of electricity. Each kilowatt-hour emits about 100 grams of CO₂, about a fifth of the world average.

Since 2000

Since 2000, wind rose from about 10% to about 60%, coal fell from about 45% to almost nothing and gas fell from about a quarter to almost nothing of the mix.

Trade and resources

Denmark is a net importer: about 20% of the electricity it uses comes from abroad.

  • Sunshine: about half the world average.
  • Rainfall: about two-thirds of the world average.
  • Biggest electricity trade partners: Germany, Sweden, Norway.
  • Main fuel supplier: the United States.
