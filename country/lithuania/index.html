How Lithuania makes its electricity

Lithuania generated about 8.9 TWh of electricity in 2025. About 45% came from wind, and about 20% from solar. Low-carbon sources (nuclear and renewables) supplied about 80% of the total.

The mix in 2025

  • Wind
  • Solar
  • Gas
  • Bioenergy
  • Other Fossil
  • Hydro

Each person uses close to the world average amount of electricity. Each kilowatt-hour emits about 140 grams of CO₂, about a third of the world average.

Since 2000

Since 2000, nuclear fell from about three-quarters to almost nothing, wind rose from almost nothing to about 45% and solar rose from almost nothing to about 20% of the mix.

Trade and resources

Lithuania is a net importer: about 30% of the electricity it uses comes from abroad.

  • Sunshine: about half the world average.
  • Rainfall: about half the world average.
  • Biggest electricity trade partners: Sweden, Latvia, Poland.
  • Main fuel supplier: Saudi Arabia.
