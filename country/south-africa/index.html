How South Africa makes its electricity

South Africa generated about 240 TWh of electricity in 2025. About 80% came from coal, and about 8% from solar. Low-carbon sources (nuclear and renewables) supplied about 20% of the total.

The mix in 2025

  • Coal
  • Solar
  • Wind
  • Nuclear
  • Other Fossil
  • Hydro

Each person uses close to the world average amount of electricity. Each kilowatt-hour emits about 700 grams of CO₂, about 1.5 times the world average.

Since 2000

Since 2000, coal fell from about 95% to about 80% and solar rose from almost nothing to about 8% of the mix.

Trade and resources

South Africa is a net exporter: it sells about 2% of what it generates to its neighbours.

  • About 90% of people have electricity at home.
  • Sunshine: a little more than the world average.
  • Rainfall: about half the world average.
  • Biggest electricity trade partners: Mozambique, Namibia, Botswana.
  • Main fuel supplier: Oman.
