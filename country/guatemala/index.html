How Guatemala makes its electricity

Guatemala generated about 16 TWh of electricity in 2024. About 40% came from hydro, and about 20% from bioenergy. Low-carbon sources (nuclear and renewables) supplied about two-thirds of the total.

The mix in 2024

  • Hydro
  • Bioenergy
  • Coal
  • Other Fossil
  • Wind
  • Other Renewables
  • Solar

Each person uses about a quarter of the world average amount of electricity. Each kilowatt-hour emits about 300 grams of CO₂, about two-thirds of the world average.

Since 2000

Since 2000, other fossil fuels fell from about 40% to about 15%, bioenergy rose from about 8% to about 20% and coal rose from about 8% to about 15% of the mix.

Trade and resources

Guatemala is a net importer: about 3% of the electricity it uses comes from abroad.

  • About 100% of people have electricity at home.
  • Sunshine: close to the world average.
  • Rainfall: about twice the world average.
  • Biggest electricity trade partners: Mexico, El Salvador, Nicaragua.
  • Main fuel supplier: the United States.
