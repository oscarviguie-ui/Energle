How Ireland makes its electricity

Ireland generated about 31 TWh of electricity in 2025. About half came from gas, and about 40% from wind. Low-carbon sources (nuclear and renewables) supplied about half of the total.

The mix in 2025

  • Gas
  • Wind
  • Solar
  • Bioenergy
  • Other Fossil
  • Hydro

Each person uses about twice the world average amount of electricity. Each kilowatt-hour emits about 260 grams of CO₂, about half the world average.

Since 2000

Since 2000, wind rose from about 1% to about 40%, coal fell from about 30% to almost nothing and other fossil fuels fell from about a quarter to about 3% of the mix.

Trade and resources

Ireland is a net importer: about 15% of the electricity it uses comes from abroad.

  • Sunshine: about half the world average.
  • Rainfall: close to the world average.
  • Biggest electricity trade partners: the United Kingdom.
  • Main fuel supplier: the United Kingdom.
