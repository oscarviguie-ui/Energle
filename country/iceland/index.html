How Iceland makes its electricity

Iceland generated about 19 TWh of electricity in 2024. About 70% came from hydro, and about 30% from other renewables. Low-carbon sources (nuclear and renewables) supplied almost all of the total.

The mix in 2024

  • Hydro
  • Other Renewables

Each person uses about 13 times the world average amount of electricity. Each kilowatt-hour emits about 30 grams of CO₂, less than a tenth of the world average.

Since 2000

Since 2000, hydro fell from about 85% to about 70% and other renewables rose from about 15% to about 30% of the mix.

Trade and resources

Iceland makes roughly as much electricity as it uses, with little net trade.

  • Sunshine: about half the world average.
  • Rainfall: about twice the world average.
  • Main fuel supplier: Norway.
